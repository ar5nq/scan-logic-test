4 \u00b7 Backtest (real win-rate numbers, runs right here \u2014 no Node needed)

Walk-forward test: replays ~2 years of hourly bars per instrument, and at every step only lets the scorer see data up to that point (no peeking at the future \u2014 that's the only honest way to backtest). Whenever it would've printed STRONG, it checks 4h/8h/24h later whether price actually moved the predicted direction. This takes a few minutes and does real work \u2014 the tab stays responsive but don't close it mid-run.

"Win" = price moved the predicted direction by that horizon \u2014 this is a directional-accuracy check, not a P&L backtest (no stop/target/spread/slippage modeled). Instruments with under ~15\u201320 STRONG calls don't have a reliable sample yet; don't market a number built on a handful of calls.

5 \u00b7 R-multiple backtest (real stop/target, not just direction)

For every STRONG call: stop = the recent swing point that defines structure (the level that would invalidate the setup), target = 2R (twice the risk distance), max hold 48h. Whichever hits first wins; if neither hits in 48h, closes at market and counts whatever R that is. This is the test that actually answers "would this have made money," not just "was the direction right." Reuses history already fetched in Section 4 if you ran that first \u2014 saves time and proxy requests.

Avg R above 0 across enough trades = the strategy has a real edge even without a great directional hit rate, since wins (2R) can outweigh losses (-1R) at well under 50%. Avg R at or below 0 = no edge with this specific stop/target scheme, even if direction alone looked ~50%.
